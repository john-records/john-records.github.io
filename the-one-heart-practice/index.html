The One Heart Practice

A Practice by John Records with Doug Matchett. A body-based contemplative practice for an open world.

Namaste

"You are holy. I see it. I bow to it."

The practice begins with the traditional greeting — hands pressed together at the heart, bowing to the holiness in another.

This is the doorway. Everything that follows moves through it.

Stage One: My Heart

Hands come to rest at the center of the chest. The practitioner turns attention inward — to their own heart, their own life, their own experience of this moment.

"This is where I am. This is what I carry."

The gesture is intimate and self-receiving. The practice begins here because it must — with the honest, embodied, present self.

Stage Two: Our Hearts

The arms open slightly, widening from the chest — an opening that includes the partner, or a held sense of those close to us. The field expands.

"Your heart matters too. We are in this together."

Stage Three: More Hearts

The arms continue to widen, now encompassing a larger circle — community, strangers, those across difference, those in suffering or joy beyond our immediate sight.

"There are more of us. The circle is wider than I usually remember."

Stage Four: Open to All Hearts

"Arms open, nothing withheld."

Stage Five: Gathering All Hearts

While lovingly sensing All Hearts, the Gatherer of Hearts — called by different names in different traditions — gently moves the arms in a bringing-together motion, and gathers all hearts into One Heart, where they have always been.

This culminates in the praying hands — the Namaste gesture — which is not other than One Heart.

"All Hearts gathered into One Heart — where they have always been."

Stage Six: The Return

The hands return to the heart. But the return is not a retreat — it is a homecoming informed by the full journey outward and back inward.

"I return to my heart — the same heart, and not the same."

Three Cycles

For those beginning the practice, three or more cycles of the full gesture sequence are recommended. Each cycle is qualitatively different — not repetition, but deepening.

With time, One Heart may perceptibly awaken in the practitioner after a single cycle — or even by the simple act of placing a hand upon the heart.

I. The Ordinary Self

The practice begins where we always begin — with the daily, embodied, somewhat distracted self. Tired from the news. Managing the to-do list. Carrying its particular griefs. This is the honest starting point.

The smaller self. The necessary beginning.

II. The Higher Self

By the second cycle, something has shifted. The act of moving through the stages has changed the one who is practicing. A more spacious self comes forward — less defended, more permeable.

Holds the ordinary, daily self with compassion.

III. The Highest Self

A third quality of presence may emerge — less "mine" in the ordinary sense, more transparent to the larger life. The place where the particular heart and the heart of all things are most nearly one.

The divine does not stand apart from the personal — it moves through it.

Theological Ground

Rooted in panentheism — the view that all things exist within the divine, and the divine within all things, while also exceeding them.

"The particular and the universal are not enemies. They are the pulse of reality."

Drawing from Hindu, Buddhist, Christian and Jewish traditions. Practitioners need not hold any of these traditions explicitly. The practice is accessible across and beyond any particular framework.

This practice is for those trying to stay open

For activists who are burning out. For contemplatives whose practice has become private. For ordinary people who want to remember, daily, that their heart belongs to a larger whole.

"Short enough to do in a few minutes. Deep enough to practice for a lifetime."
